Character Bibles: Keeping AI Film Characters Consistent

By Jeff Jones · Storyforge Imaginarium · October 28, 2026

A character bible should answer two questions: who is this person, and what must remain recognizable when the shot changes? A compelling face alone does not preserve a character’s motives, mannerisms, voice, or progression through the story.

Separate identity from scene state

Identity includes stable traits: facial structure, defining features, habitual posture, vocal qualities, values, and relationships. Scene state includes things that can change: clothing, injury, emotional intensity, carried objects, and what the character currently knows. Store these separately so a costume change does not accidentally become a different person.

Build an approved reference set

Choose a small set of approved images showing front, profile, and three-quarter views, plus a full-body reference. Include neutral lighting and enough detail to judge proportions. Label variants clearly. Ten contradictory portraits create more ambiguity than three carefully selected references.

Keep a written identity description alongside the images. Record which traits are essential and which may vary. “Silver streak at the left temple” is more testable than “mysterious and cinematic.” Reference assets should be cleared for the intended production use.

A working character-bible entry

  • Story: goal, fear, contradiction, relationships, and arc.
  • Visual identity: approved reference IDs, proportions, hair, distinctive features, and silhouette.
  • Performance: speaking rhythm, gestures, posture, and emotional range.
  • Scene states: wardrobe, props, injuries, and knowledge by scene.
  • Continuity: last approved version, change notes, and reviewer.

Test difficult shots before producing a sequence

Test the character in a close-up, a profile, a wide shot, and a different lighting condition. Compare the results side by side. If identity falls apart in profile, resolve that before building a dialogue scene that depends on profile coverage.

AI tools can still drift despite good references. Treat the bible as a review standard, not a guarantee. Reject or revise shots that contradict the approved design, and preserve the reference assets and settings used for accepted work.

Track continuity through the edit

Imagine a courier who tears her right sleeve in scene four. Later shots need the same tear until the story establishes a wardrobe change. Add the state to the shot brief and check it in the final edit, where reordered shots may introduce new inconsistencies.

Review voice and behavior too. A cautious character should not become casually reckless merely because a generated performance looks exciting. Consistency includes the choices the audience believes that person would make.

Commission a small, reviewable package

A useful paid starting scope is one principal character, an approved reference sheet, a written identity and performance guide, and a short continuity test sequence. Agree on the number of variants and revisions before production. Expand the cast after the approval process works.
