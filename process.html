From story to signed-off master

Define the work. Review the work. Deliver the film.

Before production starts, we agree the scope, budget, delivery plan, and approval process. Within that plan, short iterations let us learn from actual footage and make informed creative decisions before expensive downstream work begins.

01 · Develop the story

We assess your book, script, or creative brief, identify the audience and emotional throughline, and define what the screen adaptation must accomplish. Treatments, screenplays, pilot scripts, and character or universe bibles can be commissioned as a separate development phase.

Review output: adaptation approach, story scope, script milestones, and agreed development deliverables.

Explore book-to-screen development →

02 · Specify and plan the production

We break the approved script into scenes, sequences, and shots. Storyboards, scene blocking, character references, camera and lens direction, sound requirements, and delivery formats establish what the team will produce.

We estimate the work, assign responsibilities, identify dependencies and the critical path, reserve contingency, and set client review dates. The production baseline records scope, fees, assumptions, revision allowances, and target delivery.

Review output: production brief, shot backlog, Gantt schedule, budget, acceptance criteria, and approval responsibilities.

03 · Establish the look and prove difficult shots

We develop character, wardrobe, environment, voice, and visual references. Early tests address the shots most likely to affect feasibility, continuity, or the schedule. You review the creative direction before we expand production across a sequence.

Review output: approved references, representative shots, and recorded decisions that guide subsequent work.

04 · Produce in short, reviewable iterations

We prioritize the production backlog and assign each shot or sequence an owner, version, due date, and definition of done. Generation, editorial, voice, music, and sound advance in coordinated passes. Daily check-ins expose blockers and clarify the next commitment.

Review output: versioned review cuts, actionable notes, updated task status, and the next approval queue.

05 · Manage changes and protect the forecast

Director’s notes and new requests enter a decision log. When a request changes the agreed scope, we show the impact on cost, dependencies, and delivery before proceeding. Sponsors can approve the change, trade scope, or defer it.

We track actual and committed costs alongside the estimate to complete. Burndown and velocity help forecast remaining work; acceptance, rather than the number of generated clips, determines progress.

Review output: change requests, risk and issue log, budget forecast, and visible schedule revisions.

06 · Verify, approve, and hand over

Quality checks run through production and finishing. We review performance, character and wardrobe continuity, lighting, color, editorial flow, sound, and sync. Final checks confirm the agreed frame rate, dimensions, export formats, and delivery package.

The designated client approver signs off against the agreed acceptance criteria. A retrospective captures lessons for the next sequence, episode, or commission.

Review output: QA checklist, approval record, final masters, and agreed supporting assets.

Daily visibility for clients and project sponsors

During active production, we run a short daily stand-up and invite client stakeholders and project sponsors into the review cadence. The production team focuses on completed work, the next commitment, and blockers. Stakeholders receive a daily written progress update whether or not they attend; detailed creative reviews and sponsor decisions get focused follow-up sessions.

Each note has an owner. Each approval has a decision-maker. Any forecast slippage or scope change is made visible with its impact and a proposed next step. We agree review windows at kickoff so fast production is matched by timely decisions.

How quickly can we deliver?

We set the delivery date after reviewing scope, shot complexity, available capacity, and approval turnaround. An accelerated brief may suit a short production window; an ongoing microdrama needs an episode schedule and repeatable review cadence. Any change to the delivery forecast is reported with the cause, impact, and recovery options.

How do you maintain continuity?

Approved character and environment bibles, visual references, shot plans, and versioned assets guide the work. We check each shot against its neighbors in the edit and resolve inconsistencies before final approval.

How are commercial usage and handoff handled?

The agreement identifies intended usage, client-supplied material, third-party assets, and the licenses or permissions needed for the commission. Final delivery follows the formats and handoff requirements agreed in the brief.
